FAQ - What is Dihydrogen Monoxide?

What is Dihydrogen Monoxide?

Dihydrogen monoxide, commonly abbreviated as DHMO, is a colorless, odorless and tasteless chemical compound found virtually everywhere on Earth. It consists of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. Despite its harmless-sounding appearance, DHMO has been linked to thousands of deaths every year.

Where is DHMO used in?

  • As an industrial solvent and coolant
  • In nuclear power plants
  • In the production of Styrofoam
  • As a major ingredient in home-built bombs
  • In animal research facilities.
  • By elite athletes to enhance performance
  • It's basically everywhere. Coincidence? I don't think so.

Is DHMO dangerous?

DHMO can certainly be dangerous under certain circumstances. Exposure to large amounts can have serious consequences, and prolonged exposure to its solid form can cause tissue damage.

What happens to people who get in contact with DHMO?

So far 100% of people who have died were exposed to DHMO sooner or later. DHMO can cause near-instant death, but not necessarily always.

Can DHMO cause death?

Exposure to DHMO can be fatal in certain situations. However, the risks depend heavily on the circumstances and amount of exposure. Even small doses of DHMO can cause death if inhaled.

Why is DHMO found in drowning victims?

DHMO is frequently present in cases involving drowning. Critics argue that this deserves greater public attention.

Can DHMO cause burns?

Yes. DHMO can cause severe burns in both liquid and gas forms.

Is DHMO dangerous when frozen?

Frozen DHMO can cause injury through prolonged contact and can also create dangerous environmental conditions.
